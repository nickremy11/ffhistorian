2026 Draft Recap

The auction is settled, the ledger is $2,384 lighter, and two owners walked in and torched their keeper lists to the ground while ten others played it safer. One trade — engineered before a single bid was placed — quietly rewrote two rosters and a team name. Below: every team, who they kept, who they let walk, what they bought instead, and where the archive says this season is headed. Keeper "market value" below is each player's own ESPN preseason valuation on file — a real number, not a vibe.
🏆 Schnupper DeckersSchnupper
🏆 2 RingsDefending Champ (13–1)Kept $94
The Keep(s)

Nearly half the salary cap — $94 of $200 — went to just two names. Bijan Robinson at $72 is technically a slight overpay against his $66 market number, but nobody in this league is going to argue with re-upping the best player on a 13–1 team. Jaxon Smith-Njigba at $22 against a $64 market value is the single biggest keeper heist of the entire draft — a $42 discount on a true WR1.

Let Walk

Cut a twice-kept Josh Allen loose at his $38 keeper number rather than pay the streak tax a third time — Allen re-entered the market and Brooks scooped him up for $27.

Draft Day

With only $106 left, still landed DeVonta Smith ($27), David Montgomery ($21), and Mike Evans ($13) — a lean, efficient build around two stars who were already discounted into the roster.

Outlook

Coming off the best record in the league and running it back with an unfair-value WR2, this is the team to beat again — and everyone knows it. The path back to the title runs through a healthy Bijan and a schedule where Smith-Njigba is basically playing on a rookie contract. If there's a soft spot, it's the $106 left to build four running backs and a defense around two stars — one injury to either keeper and the depth chart gets thin fast.

🔄 Warren PeaceRemy
🏆 1 Ring2025: 2ndKept $59
The Keep(s)

The headline move of the whole offseason: before the draft, Remy and Joe swapped keeper rights outright — Remy's Tetairoa McMillan for Joe's Kenneth Walker III — each riding off with a discounted keeper instead of the guy they'd actually rostered. Walker landed at $38, and Chase Brown followed at $21 against a $41 market value, a combined $22 of surplus locked in before the auction even opened.

Let Walk

Cut A.J. Brown loose at a $54 keeper cost (he landed with Hickey for $35 in the open market) and passed on a $46 De'Von Achane, who Brooks paid $58 to bring back — more than Remy would've owed to keep him.

Draft Day

Backfield locked, so the rest of the $141 went straight to receivers: Nico Collins ($37), Malik Nabers ($36), Ladd McConkey ($27). Seven wide receivers on the roster — an embarrassment of WR1 depth behind a two-headed backfield.

Outlook

Worst-to-first-to-runner-up: last in 2023, champion in 2024, second in 2025 — the most consistent upward climb of any roster in the league, and now backed by a trade most owners wish they'd thought of first. A backfield this deep behind seven viable receivers means byes and injuries barely dent the lineup; the only thing standing between Warren Peace and a second ring is whoever's sitting in the Schnupper Deckers' chair.

🛻 Joe Der-teyJoe
🏆 1 Ring2025: 9thKept $25
The Keep(s)

The other side of the trade: parted with Kenneth Walker III to bring in Tetairoa McMillan at a $19 keeper price ($28 market value) plus Luther Burden III at $6 against a $13 number.

Let Walk

Cut a three-time-kept Nico Collins loose at a fair $37 keeper cost (no gouging, just a decision) — and Nico went straight to Remy's roster for the same $37 on the open market. Also passed on Trey McBride (2x kept, $36 cost) and a pricey CeeDee Lamb ($74 keeper cost).

Draft Day

With $175 in the bank, went hunting for stars: Ja'Marr Chase for $72 — bought on the open market from Cullen, who'd just cut him loose at a $68 keeper cost, so Joe paid all of $4 more than Cullen would have to keep his own guy. Also added Travis Etienne Jr. ($38) and Colston Loveland ($23). Seven receivers, the heaviest WR corps in the league alongside Warren Peace.

Outlook

Three straight forgettable finishes (8th, 11th, 9th) make this the league's most reliably average team — and a $72 alpha receiver plus a shrewd, discount trade for a blue-chip rookie is a real swing at relevance. The receiver room, seven deep, can mask almost any bust; the question is whether Chase, McMillan, and Loveland all stay on the field at once long enough to matter in December, because this roster has broken hearts before.

🐦 Clemson QuailmenJeremy
🏆 2 Rings2025: 4thKept $92
The Keep(s)

Kept Jahmyr Gibbs for the third straight year — at $73, the escalator has finally caught up to his $67 market value, so the discount is basically gone. Rashee Rice at $19 against a $47 market number is still an excellent number.

Let Walk

Cut Kyren Williams loose at a $39 keeper cost; he landed with Hickey for $42 on the open market — a fresh buyer paying more than Jeremy would've owed to keep his own player.

Draft Day

Only $108 left after the keepers, and still bid Justin Jefferson up to $57. Gibbs, Rice, and Jefferson alone eat $149 of the $200 cap — a true three-star build with thirteen dart-throws behind it.

Outlook

On pure talent, Gibbs/Rice/Jefferson might be the best top-three in the league — three genuine difference-makers on one roster is rare even in a keeper format. The risk is just as obvious: with $149 of the cap tied up in three names, one injury turns a title contender into a scramble for waiver-wire replacement value, and there's little left over to fix it in-season.

🦸 Zero to HeroHickey
🏆 3 Rings2025: 12th (3–11)Kept $59
The Keep(s)

Omarion Hampton at $41 is a slight overpay on a $38 market number, and Jaylen Waddle at $18 landed exactly at market — neither a steal nor a mistake, just two guys re-upped at fair price.

Draft Day

Doubled down after two straight last-place finishes, adding Kyren Williams for $42 — not the top of a running back market that saw McCaffrey go for $63, Achane for $58, and Henry for $57, but a real bell-cow addition all the same — plus A.J. Brown ($35, cut loose by Remy) and Tee Higgins ($23). $83 combined on Hampton and Williams alone is a bet the backfield can carry this out of the cellar.

Outlook

A three-time champion that's finished dead last in back-to-back seasons is the strangest résumé in the league. The name is "Zero to Hero" — this is either the year it means something again, or the third straight punchline. A crowded backfield (six running backs) says the plan is volume and matchups over one bell-cow bet, which at least gives this roster more paths out of the cellar than last year's 3-11 record suggested.

Hickey and Cullen are the league's only 3-ring owners, and both are coming off finishes in the bottom two. Nobody stays on top forever in this archive — and one of James's three didn't come easy, either: that 2022 crown is the same one split 50/50 with Cullen on the other card. Co equals No applies just as much on this side of the ledger.
🎯 Team DaVaughnteNathan
🏆 2 Rings2025: 5thKept $78
The Keep(s)

Jonathan Taylor at $57 (market $61) and George Pickens at $21 (market $30) are both fair-to-good numbers — Taylor especially is likely nearing the price where he stops being worth re-upping.

Draft Day

With only $122 left, still bought CeeDee Lamb for $50 — Joe's own keeper-options sheet had Lamb pegged at a $74 cost to keep, so getting him $24 under that on the open market is real value. Added Garrett Wilson ($25, cut loose by Bass for the exact same number) too — and, by design or accident, basically bought a Cowboys season-ticket package: Pickens, Lamb, Dak Prescott, and Jake Ferguson are all on this roster, four pieces of one NFL offense riding on a single fantasy team. Eight running backs round it out — the heaviest RB hoard in the league, a volume-over-flash approach at the position.

Outlook

A steady climb — 8th, then 5th — and now a Taylor/Pickens/Lamb/Wilson core with RB depth to survive an injury. The Cowboys stack is either a genius correlation play — start four guys who all score off the same scoring drive — or a disaster waiting on one bad Dallas offensive week. There isn't much in between, but quietly, this is one of the better-positioned rosters in the league.

🤪 Cray's Craziest TeamCullen
🏆 3 Rings2025: 11thKept $57
The Keep(s)

James Cook III at $33 (market $54) and Brock Bowers at $24 (market $38) is arguably the best surplus-value keeper pair in the entire league — $35 of combined free value locked in before the auction even opened.

Let Walk

And yet, cut a three-time-kept Ja'Marr Chase loose at a $68 keeper cost — a cut matched in size only by Joe's own CeeDee Lamb decision ($74) and Zajdel's Amon-Ra St. Brown call (also $68). Chase didn't stay unemployed long: Joe bought him right back for $72, just $4 over what it would've cost Cullen to keep him.

Draft Day

Spent the Chase savings on Breece Hall ($40), Zay Flowers ($36), and Davante Adams ($20) — real star power layered on top of the Cook/Bowers discount.

Outlook

Fell from 3rd to 11th in a single season despite a talented roster, and now enters 2026 having let go of a receiver most of the league would kill to have at that price. Cook and Bowers give this team a cheap, productive floor, but the ceiling now depends on Hall and Flowers replacing a home-run threat who's playing for a rival this year — either addition-by-subtraction, or the decision Cullen is still explaining come October.

Cullen's 2022 ring still carries an asterisk in this site's own records — the archive quietly splits that title 50/50 with Hickey. By the transitive property, a "co-champion" is just a diplomatic way of saying not really a champion — Co equals No, as it were.
🔨 The Greensboro Mighty HammersColin
🏆 1 Ring2025: 8thKept $9
The Keep(s)

Kept exactly one player: Chris Olave for $9 against a $35 market value — a $26 discount, one of the two or three best keeper values in the whole draft (only Schnupper's Smith-Njigba and Jeremy's Rashee Rice topped it) — and still left a second keeper slot completely unused.

Let Walk

Cut a twice-kept Saquon Barkley loose at a $65 keeper cost (a defensible pass at that escalating price); Barkley landed with Zajdel for $56 on the open market.

Draft Day

With $191 of freedom, went shopping for bell-cows: Derrick Henry ($57), Jeremiyah Love ($47), Emeka Egbuka ($28) — three premium buys and a five-running-back, ground-and-pound build.

Outlook

A slow climb (11th, 10th, 8th) meets real cap flexibility and legitimate bell-cow talent. Henry and Love give this roster two different paths to twenty touches a week, and Olave quietly graduates into a true WR1 role behind them — on paper, one of the more complete builds outside the top tier, if the record ever catches up to it.

☕ Fair Trade Coffee BlackBass
🏆 1 Ring2025: 6thKept $10
The Keep(s)

Kept just Quinshon Judkins for $10 against a $24 market value — a solid discount, but only one of two available slots used.

Let Walk

Cut Garrett Wilson loose at a $25 keeper cost, and he landed with Nathan's Team DaVaughnte for the exact same $25 on the open market days later — a rare case of a cut player fetching precisely what it would've cost his original owner to keep him. Also passed on Christian McCaffrey ($66 cost), who Austin bought back for $63.

Draft Day

With $190 in the bank, made a real statement bid: Puka Nacua for $62 — the third-priciest bid of the entire draft, behind only Ja'Marr Chase ($72, bought by Joe) and Christian McCaffrey ($63, bought by Austin). Also added Drake London ($44) and Josh Jacobs ($33).

Outlook

A steady 5th–6th–2nd–6th tier team that just swung for the fences at receiver. Nacua and London give this roster two legitimate WR1s, and Jacobs adds a real bell-cow behind them — if the receivers stay on the field, this is a top-half offense; if not, $62 is an expensive lesson.

🧵 Some Thomas's r Worth KeepingBrooks
🏆 1 Ring2025: 10thKept $15
The Keep(s)

Kept just Rome Odunze for $15 against a $17 market value — fair, unremarkable, and only one of two slots used. For a team whose whole name is a pun about players worth keeping, keeping one guy at a wash price is a little on the nose.

Let Walk

Cut Puka Nacua loose at a $61 keeper cost — essentially even with his $65 market value — and Bass immediately paid $62 to bring him in, practically a free assist to a division rival.

Draft Day

Still spent big: De'Von Achane for $58 was the second-priciest non-keeper running back bought all draft, trailing only Christian McCaffrey's $63, plus Trey McBride ($33), D'Andre Swift ($30), and Josh Allen ($27, fresh off Schnupper's cut list).

Outlook

A 10th-place finish undersells this roster — Achane, McBride, Swift, and Allen behind Odunze reads like a top-half team on paper. Achane's health has been the swing factor here for two years running; get a full season out of him and this is a playoff team, no asterisk required.

🧺 Mondre and D'Andre's LaundryZajdel
🏆 1 Ring2025: 3rdKept $0
The Keep(s)

None. Nine players were eligible — including Amon-Ra St. Brown, Saquon Barkley, and a three-time-kept Drake London — and every single one got cut loose. The laundry got fully washed this offseason. The only other team to blank on keepers entirely this year is Austin's — two very different squads making the exact same bet in the same offseason.

Draft Day

Turned around and bought most of them right back: Amon-Ra St. Brown for $61 (just $7 under his old $68 keeper price), Saquon Barkley for $56 (a real $9 discount off his $65 keeper number). Then got aggressive on Javonte Williams at $45 — a huge jump from the $8 keeper cost that was on file for him, a real bet that last year's breakout was the floor and not the ceiling.

Outlook

Coming off a 3rd-place finish with nearly the same core rebuilt at a small discount, the real swing factor is Javonte Williams — pay RB1 money and get RB1 production, or spend the year explaining why the laundry didn't come out any cleaner. Zajdel's title résumé, one ring at 6-8, says this owner can win with less than the eye test suggests.

The team name has quietly run a three-year rap-feud bit — "Drake Type Beat" (2023) → "Drake vs Hendrick Lamar Mixtape" (2024) → this year's laundry pun. The 2023 title, notably, came at 6–8 — the shakiest ring in league history.
🧨 1738 68iou1Austin
Ringless2025: 7thKept $0
The Keep(s)

None — and this was a loaded list. Ashton Jeanty ($58 cost, right at market), Justin Jefferson ($63 cost, still a discount), and a twice-kept Terry McLaurin were all cut loose, walking away from $230 of keeper value. The only other team to blank on keepers entirely this year is Zajdel's — two cellar-to-mid squads making the exact same bet in the same offseason.

Draft Day

Took all $200 to the open market and mostly bought his own stuff back: Christian McCaffrey for $63 (from Bass, who'd just cut him) and Ashton Jeanty for $55 — $3 less than his own keeper price would've been. Also added Carnell Tate ($14).

Outlook

The only owner in Elite FFL history with zero championships enters 2026 with zero keepers too — either the reset that finally breaks through, or more proof a fresh start alone doesn't fix a roster. McCaffrey and Jeanty give this team a real 1-2 punch if either stays healthy, which has been the exact problem for both of their careers. At least the record's trending the right way: 10th, then 9th, then 7th — thirteen seasons in, the breakthrough might finally be close.
